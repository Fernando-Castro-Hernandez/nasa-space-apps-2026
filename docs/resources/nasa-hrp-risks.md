# Risks of Human Spaceflight (NASA HRP)

- **Source:** https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/
- **Page last updated:** 2026-07-21
- **Extracted:** 2026-11-14. Short quotes are verbatim; the rest is paraphrased.

**Purpose:** The HRP "seeks to understand specific risks associated with the hazards of human spaceflight." The page lists the health and performance risks HRP has identified, each with a link to a detail page. Their effects vary depending on whether the mission goes to low Earth orbit, the Moon or Mars.

The page groups 17 risks into three categories. It does not map each risk to a specific hazard.

## Category 1: Human physiology

| #   | Risk                                                  | Effect (per page)                                                                                     | Research focus / countermeasure                                           |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Altered Immune System Responses                       | Stress can reactivate dormant viruses, causing rashes and other symptoms.                             | Understand immune changes to protect crew health.                         |
| 2   | Altered Sensorimotor and Vestibular Function          | Motion sickness, poor hand-eye and gait coordination, dizziness during gravity changes.               | Faster recovery so crews can work inside and outside the spacecraft.      |
| 3   | Cardiovascular Adaptations                            | Weightlessness alters circulation and heart/vessel function; may raise risk of cardiovascular events. | Minimize these changes.                                                   |
| 4   | Decompression Sickness                                | Moving to a lower-pressure suit can form nitrogen bubbles in tissues.                                 | Better prevention and faster treatment.                                   |
| 5   | Host-Microorganism Interactions                       | Skin and gut microbiomes change in space; may increase illness.                                       | Study how these microbial ecosystems evolve.                              |
| 6   | Hypoxia                                               | Steps to avoid "the bends" on spacewalks raise risk of mild hypoxia (low tissue oxygen).              | —                                                                         |
| 7   | Inadequate Food and Nutrition                         | A balanced diet is essential to health and performance.                                               | Nutritious, long-lasting meals for deep space; effects of poor nutrition. |
| 8   | Injury and Compromised Performance from Spacewalks    | Cognitive fatigue, injury, decompression sickness, low oxygen, high CO₂.                              | Characterize performance in suits; balance pressure and oxygen.           |
| 9   | Reduced Cardiorespiratory and Musculoskeletal Fitness | Losses in bone density, muscle strength and cardiovascular performance.                               | All astronauts exercise; find which exercise protects best.               |
| 10  | Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS)   | Structure of eyes and brain can change.                                                               | Understand long-term consequences and prevent changes.                    |
| 11  | Spaceflight-Induced Bone Changes                      | Bones break down and become brittle; higher fracture risk.                                            | Preserve bone through diet, exercise and medication.                      |

Image caption on the page: Visual Impairment Intracranial Pressure (VIIP) Syndrome "was identified in 2005" and is "currently NASA's leading spaceflight-related health risk," more prevalent in men than women. The page does not say explicitly that VIIP and SANS are the same condition.

## Category 2: Behavioral performance and human factors

| #   | Risk                                         | Effect (per page)                                                              | Research focus                                            |
| --- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 12  | Behavioral Changes and Psychiatric Disorders | Isolation, radiation, distance and other stressors may affect mental health.   | Understand and quantify psychological risks.              |
| 13  | Inadequate Teamwork                          | Crew compatibility is essential to safety and mission success.                 | Team function in isolation; miscommunication and tension. |
| 14  | Inadequate Sleep and Irregular Schedules     | Chronic sleep loss, constant workload, disrupted biological clocks.            | Reduce these stressors' effects on health and safety.     |
| 15  | Inadequate Systems Operations Support        | Unexpected problems with software, vehicles or systems on deep space missions. | What information crews need, when, and what training.     |
| 16  | Injury from Dynamic Loads                    | Injury during landing.                                                         | Likelihood and prevention.                                |

## Category 3: Space radiation

| #   | Risk                      | Effect (per page)                               | Research focus                                                      |
| --- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 17  | Radiation-Induced Cancers | Exposure may increase the likelihood of cancer. | Best mitigations and interventions ("ongoing research priorities"). |

## Not on the page

- No exposure limits, dose thresholds, likelihood or severity ratings.
- No specific countermeasure protocols beyond the research focus above.
- No evidence books or data tables. That detail lives in the linked risk pages.
