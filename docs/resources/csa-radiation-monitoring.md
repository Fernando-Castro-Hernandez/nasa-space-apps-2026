# Monitoring Radiation for Astronaut Health (CSA)

- **Source:** https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp
- **Published:** 2026-02-26
- **Extracted:** 2026-11-14. Short quotes are verbatim; the rest is paraphrased.
- Non-U.S. Government site: follow its own data use terms.

**Purpose:** Announces CSA funding for the Canadian Active Neutron Spectrometer (CANS). Radiation is presented as one of the key challenges for astronauts working beyond the protection of Earth's atmosphere, as Canada and its partners plan Moon and Mars missions.

## Key content

- **Funding:** "$5.5 million to Bubble Technology Industries for the continued development of the CANS."
- **Instrument:** CANS is "a compact and autonomous instrument designed to measure neutron radiation exposure in space." It works continuously and autonomously, an improvement over earlier detection methods.
- **Neutron radiation:** "contributes to about 30% of total radiation on the ISS." It is "especially harmful" because it can cause serious biological damage and requires specialized protective measures.
- **ISS as test bed:** For over 25 years. Canadian radiation experiments on the ISS include MOSFET, EVARM, and Radi-N / Radi-N2.
- **Predecessor:** Bubble detectors, used on the ISS by CSA astronaut David Saint-Jacques, are "a predecessor to Canadian Active Neutron Spectrometer (CANS)."
- **Purpose of the data:** "could help develop better measures to reduce risks for astronauts' health."
- **Earth applications:** cancer therapy, radiation protection for aircrews, nuclear threat detection and public safety.

## Not on the page

- No doses, dose rates, exposure values or limits.
- No health effects beyond "serious biological damage."
- No mission durations or specific countermeasures.
- No technical specs for CANS, MOSFET, EVARM or Radi-N.
